# CFP to GO: Essential Speaker Cheatsheet

**Summary:**  
A short, practical guide for writing and submitting a great CFP — now including ready-to-use AI prompts to speed up your workflow.

---

## 1. Context: The Myth vs. The Reality

**Why Submit?**  
- **Myth:** Submitting is just for fame.  
- **Reality:** Submission helps with personal growth, branding, and career opportunities.

**Speaker Profile**  
- **Myth:** Conferences want only “rockstar” speakers.  
- **Reality:** Conferences want good ideas. Content > Persona.

**Talk Readiness**  
- **Myth:** The talk must be ready before submitting.  
- **Reality:** You only need a clear idea. Slides are created after acceptance.

**Topic Originality**  
- **Myth:** It must be groundbreaking.  
- **Reality:** A familiar topic with a fresh angle can be even better.

**Abstract Length**  
- **Myth:** Longer is better.  
- **Reality:** Short, sharp, focused abstracts win. Reviewers skim.

**Tailoring**  
- **Myth:** One abstract fits all conferences.  
- **Reality:** Tailoring each submission significantly increases acceptance chances.

---

## 2. CFP Submission Strategy & Tips

### A. The 3 Ingredients of a Good Proposal

1. **The Hook:**  
   Why this topic matters right now.

2. **Pain → Promise:**  
   - Pain example: “Unreadable code.”  
   - Promise example: “Three psychological tricks to fix it.”

3. **The Outcome:**  
   What practical skill attendees will gain.  
   Example: “Reduce code review time by 40%.”

---

### B. The 4-Sentence Abstract Formula

1. **The Hook:** “Most devs struggle with…”  
2. **The Pain:** “The real challenge is…”  
3. **The Action:** “In this talk, we will…”  
4. **The Takeaway:** “You will leave knowing…”

---

### C. Bio and Title Tips

#### Title Patterns That Work
- “X without the pain of Y”  
- “How I used A to fix B”  
- “Why X has nothing to do with Y”

#### The Bio Formula
1. Job title  
2. Credibility point (e.g., “Scaled DBs to 100M users”)  
3. Why you’re the right person for this topic  
4. Short fun fact or hobby

---

### D. What Judges Look For (and Avoid)

**Positive Indicators**
- Original angle  
- Practical value  
- Clear promise  
- No sales pitch  

**CFP Killers**
- Buzzword soup  
- Too generic  
- Passive phrasing (“I will talk about…”)  
- Typos

## 3. AI Prompts to Improve Your CFP Workflow

Below are two optimized prompts you can use with any LLM to accelerate your CFP creation process.

---

### 📌 **CFP Helper Prompt (Generate Title + Abstract)**

```text
You are an expert conference content writer specializing in creating captivating CFPs (Call for Papers) for tech conferences.
Your task is to take a short idea or description of a talk and transform it into a complete CFP submission.

Please generate:

1. A catchy and memorable title (that would grab attention on a conference agenda).
2. A talk description (5–8 sentences) following this structure:

   Hook: Start with an engaging or surprising statement that draws the reader in.
   Context: Briefly explain what the talk is about and why it matters.
   Details: Provide a concise overview of what the talk will cover (technologies, concepts, or problems solved).
   Takeaway: End with what the audience will gain or be able to do after attending the talk.

Keep the tone professional yet energetic, suitable for a modern tech conference.
Avoid buzzwords or filler text — make it sound authentic, practical, and inspiring.
Output in English.

Example Input:
"Using Node.js at the Edge to process user data locally before sending it to the cloud."

Example Output:
Title: "Node on the Edge: No More Waiting for the Cloud!"
Description:
What if your code could react to users instantly—before the cloud even gets involved? In this session, we’ll explore...

```

# 🧭 Outline Helper

**Prompt:**

```
I am preparing a conference talk. Here are the details:
1. Talk Title: [Insert title]
2. Time Available: [minutes]
3. Abstract: [paste abstract]
4. Audience: [beginner/intermediate/advanced/mixed]
5. Tone: [funny/technical/storytelling/practical/demo-heavy]

Create an outline that includes:
- Powerful Hook (30–60 sec)
- Problem Setup
- 3–5 Core Points (mini-hook → insight → example/demo → takeaway)
- Climax Moment (aha moment)
- Resolution
- Circle-Back Ending
- Final CTA

Produce:
- Detailed time-segmented outline
- Clear narrative arc
- Notes for jokes/visuals/demos
- Strong ending line referencing the opening hook.
```

## 4. Next Steps

### After Submitting

**Do:**
- Submit to **3+ more conferences**
- Share an **“I applied!”** post on social media

**Don’t:**
- DM organizers  
- Refresh your inbox repeatedly  

---

### If You’re Declined
- It's **not personal**
- Politely ask for **one line of feedback**
- Reuse or adjust the abstract for another event

---

### If You’re Accepted
- Celebrate publicly and **tag the event**
- Start building your **slides**
